He asked for a mockup first (Claude outputs/pilot_kills_list_mockup.html) and took all four recommended answers: a ▶ button per row, the list follows the switch, keep reading older kills, columns as drawn.

What you get

  • ▶ before each hull with kills. It opens that hull's kills under the row, one row at a time. Pressing the rest of the row still picks the hull for the loss tabs, as before.
  • The list: newest first, 10 at a time, in the switch's gang size. Each kill shows:
    • when, in your time;
    • the victim's ship, class, pilot and corporation ticker;
    • the players on the kill (dot in the size colour);
    • the system;
    • their weapon on that kill;
    • who flew with them;
    • zKillboard's value;
    • a "zKillboard ↗" link to the kill.
    On a phone, each kill takes three lines.
  • Show 10 more: first the rest of the kills already read for the dossier (its newest 50), then older killmails from ESI, 25 at a time and up to 2 batches per press, until 10 more turn up in that hull and size. The line beside the button says how many of how many, then "all N … in zKillboard's list".
  • Requests: none from zKillboard (its list of their kills, with each kill's value, is already asked), plus:
    • one ESI names lookup for the victims;
    • their corporations' tickers (kept);
    • older killmails only when you ask for them (kept for good).
    The dossier's own numbers stay on its newest 50 kills.
  • ESI failing on some older kills: what was read is listed, with "N older kills couldn't be read (PILOT-09)" and a Try again button. PILOT-09 is new; ERRORS.md has 40 codes.
  • No number the engine works out changed.

Lesson for future models

LESSON #34, from the other side: a check that works out a value the page formats with toFixed must round the JavaScript way. The check's Python f'{x:.1f}' sent 51.25 to "51.2" (ties to even) where the page showed "51.3". When a check and the page differ on an exact half, look at the check's rounding first.
