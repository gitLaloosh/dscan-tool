Where a report is, and the Intel switch

IntelUI 2026-10-30 · v2026.10.30.2

Why

  • A pilot read as the system. The owner's screenshot showed a line of three pilots and a system, where the first pilot's name is also a real system's. The row was headed by that "system", 26 jumps away; the real system, 13 jumps away, became a second chip. The owner: "the tool recorded a pilot as the system name even though a valid system name was in the entry", and "some players actually name their pilots in a way that it will screw up the intel channel entries. let's them navigate enemy space w/out actually being detected".
  • The switch. From users: "the intel on/off blends in too much. First time in took me a minute to figure out how to start it".
  • The info panel. From a user, about "How Intel works": "Bro you gotta find a way to condense this". It was 597 words, the longest of the page's 25 panels.

Where a report is

The cause: the reader took any Title Case word matching a system name as a system before any name was looked up, and a word taken as a system was never checked as a pilot. Now, when a line names two or more systems, the choice is made after the lookups, in the order the owner set out:

  1. What the words around a name say ("evaluate what they're saying"). "Y gate" is a gate in another system, "-> Y" or "heading Y" is where they're going, "from Y" is where they came from. "jumped to Y" is where they are.
  2. The ring ("if it's not inside the ring, dont consider it a valid report"). The ring is the Feed's Distance, or the alert range if that's larger. With Distance "any" there's no ring.
  3. A name that is also a pilot's becomes the pilot, as long as another system is left.
  4. The nearer one. When the distances are equal or unknown, the system name that is the first or last word of the line wins. Position only breaks a tie (the owner: "dont blindly do it").

A system left over shows as a note after the one they're in: "or Y?", "on Y gate?" when the two are next to each other, "on Y gate", "→ Y" or "from Y". There is never a second row, and nothing is dropped. A line with only one system keeps it, whatever else the word could be.

Linked words. The chat log marks a link with a * after it, with no type: the three logs checked held 119 links, mostly system names. A linked word that the reader took as an intel word, a filler word or a head count is now looked up too, and is the pilot when it is one. So a pilot named "Clear", dragged in from Local, can't mark a system clear.

Ship names stay ships. A pilot named like a ship still marks the system as seen, and dragged-in ship links would otherwise turn into pilots.

The ring and Near You (agreed as "fair"). A report outside the ring stays in the Feed and is left out of Near You, so it never alerts.

The switch

Two mockups came first. The first tried a "Start Intel" step, which the owner rejected: "that's a lot of words just to get around the actual switch not being obvious". The second moved the switch and the ⓘ around, and the owner settled on today's layout: "with the new button treatment it should pop enough".

The result, the owner's option 2: while off, the Intel switch has an amber edge, knob and "Off", and breathes slowly. It is still with reduced motion. On is the usual teal.

Only #intelSw gets this. CLAUDE.md's UI rules name it as the one exception, and check_ui_rules.py fails if any other switch gets an off state or animation of its own, or if this one moves without a reduced-motion rule.

How Intel works, condensed

The panel now has three short paragraphs with bold leads (start, Near You, Feed), and everything else under six folded headings. 96 words show instead of 597. CARD_INFO gained two forms, drawn by infoParaHtml: [Heading] text and **Lead:** text. check_subtext.py checks both forms.

It also drops what was out of date in the old text: "the slider" (it's a switch), "a red row" (the amber edge since v2026.10.30.1), and a friendly-only kill "without an alert" (an enemy killed near you alerts since v2026.10.30.1).

Lesson for future models

LESSON #105: a reader that decides a word's kind before its lookups can't use what they say. Keep both readings until the lookups are back, and break ties toward the reading that warns. In a check, make the mock answer for the ambiguous word.
