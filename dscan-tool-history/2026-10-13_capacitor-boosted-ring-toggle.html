Capacitor ring gauge + boosted/unboosted toggle

New featureRequested by the owner 2026-10-13

Request

the owner felt the Capacitor card wasn't realistically representing cap integrity for fits that use a Capacitor Booster, an Ancillary Shield Booster, or similar charge-fed boost modules: the plain "Drains dry in X" text treated every cap-draining fit the same, whether or not it actually had a way to replenish. He asked for a visual ring (similar to the in-game capacitor gauge) in place of that text, with a small toggle fixture that flips the reading between unboosted and boosted, defaulting ON in the Capacitor card. In the Weaknesses card, the existing cap-weak flag should keep its ring visual but only fire when the fit is unstable even with boosters assumed on — he explicitly didn't want a module flagged as a weakness just because a killmail/D-scan snapshot didn't happen to show a charge loaded (a fit may have simply used up all its charges in the fight that killed it). Design settled across a short back-and-forth:

  • Toggle snaps to the best-case assumption when no charge is confirmed anywhere in the fit (module, cargo, fleet hangar) — "assume the opponent is at their best rather than underestimating."
  • Toggle defaults OFF only when there's no confirmed charge, and the card says plainly that it's defaulting off because no charge was found.
  • Cargo-inference was asked to also check Fleet Hangar, not just Cargo — turned out to already be the case for the existing Capacitor Booster fallback, so this was free to extend to the new mechanism.
  • Dropped the old "stable but thin margin" trigger entirely for the Weaknesses-card flag — it was a separate, more conservative threshold layered on top of real instability, and the owner wanted it gone for now.
  • Wording: never "drain dry" or "drain-dry" — "drain" or "drained" only.
  • Whatever numbers the ring/toggle reflects (net cap rate, active drain) must themselves change when toggled, not just the headline stability text.
  • A genuinely distinct question was raised and deliberately deferred rather than folded in here: should the Fit Analyzer (EFT paste, evaluating a fit directly rather than a killmail snapshot) flag a cap-booster-capable module with no charges found as an actual weakness, since there's no "it probably had charges when it died" alibi for a fit someone is designing right now? Logged as its own backlog item (dscan-tool.md #2) rather than implemented — this build applies the uniform "assume best case" logic to both the killmail and EFT-paste paths.

Research

Live-verified via everef.net that the mechanism genuinely differs between two module types that both "boost" the capacitor:

ModuleMechanismNotes
Capacitor BoosterInjects GJ per charge cycle (capacitorBonus, attr 67)Own capacitorNeed is 0 by construction. No chargeSize gate on the module — only bounded by the module's own charge-bay capacity (attr 38) vs. the charge's volume. Gain genuinely varies by which of 18 real charge types (9 GJ tiers × 2 name variants) is loaded, so the no-data fallback needs a real min/max range, not a single assumed value.
Ancillary Shield BoosterPays its own activation cost from the loaded charge instead of ship capacitorIts own capacitorNeed is a real, large, always-counted baseline drain (Capital ASB: 4,000 GJ/5s = 800 GJ/s) that was previously counted against the ship with no way to exclude it. Loads the same Capacitor Booster Charge group (87) as the dedicated module, but gated by chargeSize (attr 128) — and its exclusion amount is binary (charge present or not), not size-dependent, since a bigger charge just lasts longer rather than excluding more GJ/s.

Cross-checked the Large Ancillary Armor Repairer for comparison (type 33103): its charge mechanism changes repair amount (×3), not where the cap cost is drawn from — confirming AAR correctly stays outside this new mechanism, already covered by the existing armor-repair logic.

Implementation

Added a confidence-tiered model inside estimateHpAndDps(): confirmed (a charge flag-paired to the module, i.e. actually loaded on the snapshot), inferred (a matching spare charge found in Cargo or Fleet Hangar — reused the Capacitor Booster fallback's existing ['Cargo', 'Fleet hangar'] scan rather than writing a new one, since it already covered both buckets), and assumed (best-case snap when a boost-capable module is fitted but nothing is found anywhere). The two mechanisms are combined into one pair of numbers per tier — combinedConfident (confirmed + inferred charges) and combinedAssumed (best case) — each re-running simulateCapacitorStability() with the relevant drain subtracted back out, so the ring and the Weaknesses-card flag share the same underlying computation.

Capacitor card: capacitorCardHtml() now tracks persisted per-fit state (f.capBoostOn, defaulting ON only when a confident tier exists), renders a 64px ring (capRingHtml(), stablePct as fill when stable, time-to-empty as centered text when not) with a small bolt-icon toggle fixture anchored to its corner (cyan when confidently boosted, amber when assumed, plain when off), and a caption explaining what's driving the number currently shown. "Net cap rate" and the active-drain figure both swap to the active tier's values when toggled, per the owner's explicit requirement.

Weaknesses card: dropped the old thin-margin trigger (stablePct < 25%) entirely. The flag now fires only when the fit is unstable even assuming every available boost mechanism is active at best case — so a fit that merely looks thin but is actually stable no longer gets flagged, and a fit that's genuinely draining gets a caveat noting the figure already assumes any fitted booster has charges. Wording fixed throughout ("drains"/"drained", never "drains dry").

Icons follow the file's existing accessibility convention (a <title> child element, not a title="" attribute, plus a transparent hit-rect for stroke-only glyphs). The toggle's click handler mirrors the pre-existing wireTacticalModeButtons() pattern (mutate state on the fit object → full innerHTML re-render of the card stack via renderFitSummaryHtml() → re-wire both the TD-mode buttons and the cap-boost toggle, regardless of which one triggered the re-render, since the whole card stack gets replaced either way) — wired at both existing call sites, the Fit Analyzer's single-fit render and the Pilot Analyzer's multi-loss tabs (each tab keeps its own independent toggle state, same as its own independent TD-mode selection).
