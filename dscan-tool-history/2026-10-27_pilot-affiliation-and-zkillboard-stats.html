What you get

  • Pilot Affiliation (aff), at the top right of the Pilot Analyzer from 1241 px (beside the inputs and the pilot line; the loss tabs start below it), under the pilot line on narrower screens.
  • Corporation and alliance with logos and tickers; Your Side's tag (Friendly / Hostile and why) moved into the box from the pilot line; In corp since (their corporation history), Pilot since (the character's birth year), Sec (security status to one decimal).
  • zKillboard, all time: Danger (zKillboard's dangerous / snuggly %), Gang (solo % = 100 − gang %, and their average gang size), kills, losses and ISK destroyed, and a link to their zKillboard page. zKillboard's numbers, shown as it gives them (DEVIATIONS.md section 3).
  • Requests: ESI /characters/{id}/ and /characters/{id}/corporationhistory/ (kept an hour), names and tickers as the System Analyzer gets them (a week); one zKillboard /api/stats/characterID/{id}/ through the zKillboard queue after the analysis' own kills and losses requests, so the analysis isn't slowed, kept an hour.
  • Failures say so in a short line with the cause on hover (so a zKillboard outage's long message isn't shown a third time): "zKillboard stats unavailable (ZKB-01 / ZKB-05)", and the new PILOT-08 when ESI's character record fails. A pilot with no zKillboard history: "No kills or losses on zKillboard." A shared killmail link or a name that isn't a pilot: no box.
  • Field names (dangerRatio, gangRatio, avgGangSize, shipsDestroyed, shipsLost, iskDestroyed) are zKillboard's stats document as other tools read it; zKillboard's API page couldn't be fetched from here, so please glance at one real pilot against their zKillboard page. A missing field leaves its line out rather than showing a wrong number.
  • No number the engine works out changed.

Lesson for future models

LESSON #77: a failure case leaves state behind. The check's zKillboard 500 put the page's zKillboard queue into its back-off, so the next case (a wrong answer, expecting ZKB-05) showed ZKB-01, and a later "no stats request" check passed whatever the page did: the queue answered from its back-off without asking (a mutant that asked anyway survived). Run each failure case in its own browser context, or put the ones that leave state behind last and say why.
