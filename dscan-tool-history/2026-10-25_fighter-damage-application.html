What pyfa does

pyfa v2.69.3, graphs/data/fitDamageStats/calc/application.py, read in the source:

  • Per ability. getApplicationPerKey takes each default-active damage ability of each squadron separately (Attack and Missiles; bombs are off by default). Lock range works as for the other weapons. There is no control-range limit like the drones'.
  • Range (getFighterAbilityMult): with the graph's default drone mode (auto), a squadron at least as fast as the target follows it and has full range. A slower one is put at the carrier's centre: distance + carrier radius − fighter radius, against the ability's range (RangeOptimal, else Range, and RangeFalloff). Missiles have no falloff, so it's a hard limit.
  • Explosion: _calcMissileFactor, with the reduction factor and sensitivity combined as log(drf) / log(drs).
  • A resistance most people don't know about. When the target is a fit, pyfa multiplies by the target ship's attribute named by the ability's ResistanceID. A ship without it gets the attribute's SDE default:
    • superiority fighters' missiles (Equite / Locust / Satyr / Gram): fighterAbilityAntiFighterMissileResistance, default 0.05, and no ship carries it. EVE University says the same ("-95% damage" to real ships);
    • heavy fighters' torpedoes (Cyclops / Malleus / Tyrfing / Mantis): fighterAbilityAntiCapitalMissileResistance, default 0.1. The 59 capital and freighter hulls carry 1.
  • Speed: the squadron's modified maxVelocity. Its MWD, afterburner and evasive abilities are off by default (Fighter.__init__), so they don't count.

Found on the way: a scripted Omnidirectional Tracking Link (drones too, since v.40)

The drone handler rows read the link's raw attributes. pyfa reads the module's modified ones, and a script changes exactly those:

  • an Optimal Range Script doubles the range / falloff bonus and zeroes the tracking and explosion bonuses;
  • a Tracking Speed Script does the reverse.

So a scripted link gave drones its unscripted bonuses. Now each scripted copy feeds its script-modified attributes (ewarAttrsFor), scripted copies first, for drones and fighters alike. A new golden fit, vexor_constructed_scripted_omni_link_drones, fails v.63: optimal 5,644 m vs pyfa's 6,037 m, tracking 0.800 vs 0.696 (LESSON #59).

Lesson for future models

LESSON #59: a generated row's "value from the carrier's attribute X" means the carrier's MODIFIED attribute, after its own script and self effects. Reading the raw one is right only until someone loads a script. When you build golden fits to cover a handler table, give every module that takes a script a scripted copy. This bug had sat unnoticed in the drone path for 24 versions, because every drone fit's link was unscripted.
