What you get

  • Pilot Dossier (dossier), open above the loss tabs: their newest 50 kills and 30 losses, per hull.
  • A switch: All / Solo / Small gang / Fleet, each with its count. Size = the player attackers on the kill (Solo 1, Small gang 2–10, Fleet 11+); NPCs don't count. Losses count toward a hull, not a size.
  • Each row: the hull and its class; "N losses · fit from their losses" or "no loss of theirs"; a bar of solo / small / fleet kills; usually N pilots; weapons seen; flies with (the other attackers' hulls, counted); kills mostly (the victims' classes); last seen. Every number a count, so thin data looks thin.
  • The rows replace the hull dropdown (D7). The busiest hull is chosen first; pressing a row shows that hull's losses below, and the pilot line names it with its class.
  • A hull they never lost: their alliance's (else corporation's) common fit for it, with a note above the tabs that it's theirs, not the pilot's ("… not their own"). An NPC corporation or no losses there: "fit unknown".
  • Footer: active hours in your own time zone (labelled, e.g. UTC+9), usual systems, solo / gang split.
  • Requests: the kills list is now asked once and shared with the Last Kill line; killmails are read from ESI (kept for good). The only new zKillboard request is the alliance's losses for a hull they never lost (one, shared and kept an hour with the fleet view's).
  • PILOT-03 now reads "No recorded kills or losses". No number the engine works out changed.

Lesson for future models

LESSON #79: removing an element removes everything it did, not only what it showed. The hull dropdown had also named the hull on the pilot line before the fit was built; its replacement label came after the fit, so a failing fit left the line empty. Before removing an element, list its side effects and where each lives now, write "what was asked" before the step that can fail, and test the failure path.
