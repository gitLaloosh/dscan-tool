Intel

How Intel works

Start: in EVE, Settings → Chat → Log Chat to File. Here: choose the Chatlogs folder, tick your intel channels, then turn on the switch beside the gear (amber while Intel is off).

Near You: systems with reports or kills within your alert range + 5 jumps, nearest first. An amber edge means within your alert range: the sound, the notification and the amber “N near” on your profile, from any tab.

Feed: every report and kill as it comes in.

How a line is read

Systems, ship names (English or Chinese), counts (+5, x2) and words like clr and nv; the rest is looked up on ESI as pilot names and coloured by your Standings. One word typed by hand is a pilot only in the name's own capitals, and report words like linked, drag, hot or kill only when dragged in from chat. A word it can't place stays plain text, never guessed. A report about the ESS tags the system in Near You: white when someone is at it, red when they're linked to it; a bubble that isn't a gate camp is a tag too. When a line names two systems, they're placed where the line says they are (“on Y gate”, “→ Y”), else in the one within range, else the nearer; the other shows as a note. The copy button on a line copies its shape, names replaced, to report a line read wrong.

Gates and camps

A short name before “gate” (“T5Z gate”, “MOG gate”) is read among the gates of the system the line is in. A report that places them on a gate and says camp, bubble, drag, smartbomb or the gate is hot tags the system in Near You with a red Camp tag and the gate. Two kills at one gate in the time set, with no such report, tag it in orange (zKillboard places each kill at the nearest stargate). A grey tag is a gate named with no camp words. “toward you”: that gate leads closer to you. Your own shorthands (word = system) go under the gear; 4 or more letters before a system's dash (“ymjg”) read as that system when only one has them.

Kills

While Intel is on, zKillboard's live feed of every kill (about 20 small requests a minute), kept within the Feed's kill range under the gear (10 jumps unless you change it), no NPC kills. A kill near you shows in Near You with who was there and alerts like a report within your alert range. A kill's label (or a badge in Near You) opens it in the Fleet tab: the victim and who killed them; a badge for several kills lists them there to pick from. The line under the title says when a kill was last read.

Near You and alerts

Everyone and every ship reported in a system in the time set under the gear (15 minutes unless you change it), until someone reports it clear. A hostile or neutral within your alert range plays the sound and notifies, once per system every 30 seconds; friendlies never alert. With the Feed's Distance set, a report beyond both it and your alert range stays out of Near You.

Jumps and your system

Your system comes from your character's Local log. Jumps are gate jumps from EVE's published stargate data (no jump bridges or wormholes); the table is checked against each new EVE build every day.

Buttons

A pilot's name opens them in the Pilot Analyzer, in their ship when the line says it. A system's name shows only that system in the Feed (press it again for everything); the radar icon beside it in Near You sends its pilots and ships to the System Analyzer; Restore my paste puts yours back.

Privacy and the folder

Nothing from your logs leaves the browser except the words looked up on ESI, and nothing is kept after you close the page. Chrome or Edge read the folder once you choose it (after a browser restart, turning Intel on asks once more); the logs are read every 2 seconds, also in a hidden tab.

Questions for you

  1. Report rings: B (colour = standing, size = gang size) or A (colour = gang size)? My pick is B: red / grey / blue already mean hostile / neutral / friendly everywhere on the page, and the routes use them too. In A, the gang-size colours (the Pilot Analyzer's cyan, amber, purple) collide with the map's cyan routes and you-are-here ring and its amber alert range, and you lose who they are at a glance.
  2. Gang sizes: the rings use the page's own three sizes (solo 1, small gang 2–10, fleet 11+, as in the Pilot Analyzer). Enough, or a fourth, bigger ring for a large fleet (say 50+)?
  3. Busy areas: at 30 jumps around Amamake the map holds about 4,000 systems. Is that still useful once you zoom, or should the map's top be lower than the Feed's largest range after all?
  4. Steppers: does the Range and alerts box read well with the three steppers (Feed, Near You, Alert), in that order?
